TRACK 01 / 11 · Intro / Ambient / Spoken
[No beat. Field recording — creek water, wind in ridge trees. Voice enters slowly.]
Route 119 is a state road.
It runs through Pike County, Kentucky,
which is in the southeastern corner of the state,
which is in the Appalachian coalfields,
which is in the oldest mountain range in the world.
The road is two lanes.
The road follows the creek.
In most places the creek is close enough
that you can hear it with the window down.
The ruts are bad in spring.
The curves are tight at the county line.
The shoulder disappears in three or four places
where the ridge comes down to the road's edge
and the road just has to accommodate it.
I've driven this road at every hour.
Coming home from gigs at two AM
with the gas light on and the crack in the windshield
catching the headlights wrong.
Going out at six AM in February
when the fog sits in the hollow
and the road disappears into it
ten yards ahead of you.
I was born in a county that has this road in it.
My grandmother was born in a county that has this road in it.
Her grandmother was born in a county
before the road was formally a road.
The creek was here first.
The road follows the creek
because the creek knew where to go
and we had the sense to follow.
[The creek sound rises. Holds. Then a beat comes in underneath it — low, patient.]
This is an album about this place.
Not about what I think of this place.
About what this place actually is.
I'm going to try to get it right.
